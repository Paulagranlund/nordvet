# No-shows at Nordvet: what the data shows

*For Mette, Head of Operations. Based on VetOne data from 6 August to 13 November 2026 (about 66,000 past appointments at 39 clinics).*

## The short version

1. **Call Odense C.** Its no-show rate has tripled since early October, from 6–8% to about 20%.
2. **Look into Hillerød, Aarhus C and København V.** For months they have been about 3× worse than the rest of the chain.
3. **Send reminders for appointments booked more than a week ahead.** This is the biggest lever across all clinics.
4. **Use the morning dashboard** to spot the next Odense C within days, not months.

## What we found

**8.5% of expected visits are no-shows, and the rate is stable.** That is about 130 lost vet-hours a week, roughly 6,900 a year if nothing changes: the time of about three full-time vets. The data has no prices, so the cost in kroner is *lost vet-hours × what a vet-hour is worth to Nordvet*.

**Three clinics stand out: Hillerød (23%), Aarhus C (22%) and København V (19%).** The rest of the chain averages 7.4%. The pattern holds for every vet, every service and every booking lead time at those clinics, so it points to how these clinics work (for example reminders or booking routines) rather than to their patients or individual vets. If they came down to the level of the other clinics, Nordvet would get back about **830 vet-hours a year**.

**Odense C is getting worse.** It went from 6–8% in August–September to 15–21% every week since early October. The rise is concentrated on two of its three vets' calendars. Something changed there around the start of October, and the clinic will know what.

**The further ahead an appointment is booked, the more likely a no-show.**

| Booked | Same day | 1–3 days | 4–7 days | 8–14 days | 15+ days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No-show rate | 3% | 7% | 9% | 9% | **17%** |

**Follow-ups (12%) and vaccinations (10%) are skipped most; surgery (3%) rarely.** Consultations lose the most hours overall because there are so many of them. Mondays (10.5%) and 8 am slots (11%) are a little worse than average.

## What we checked and ruled out

- **Not a few "serial" no-show owners.** An animal that missed its last visit is only slightly more likely to miss the next one (11% vs 8%). Penalising repeat offenders would not move the total much.
- **Not slow registration at some clinics.** Every clinic records no-shows equally late (about a day after the appointment), so comparing clinics is fair.
- **"Cancelled after the appointment time"** (about 1,100 cases, 1–2.5% at every clinic) are kept separate and not counted as no-shows. They are spread evenly, so they don't change which clinics stand out. Worth asking the clinics what these are.

## Good to know when reading the numbers

- **Yesterday's numbers are provisional.** No-shows are typically recorded about a day late, so "still unresolved" appointments can turn into no-shows over the next few days.
- **No-show rate = no-shows ÷ (completed + no-shows).** Cancelled appointments are left out, since nobody was expected.
- **Deleted appointments are excluded.** Ystad (closed in September) is included in history but not in daily reports.
